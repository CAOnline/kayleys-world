Study Notes

themes · insights · connections

Book of Enoch
The Watchers — Who Were They?
The Watchers (Aramaic: עִירִין, Irin) were 200 angels who descended to Mount Hermon under their leader Semjâzâ. They took human wives and fathered the Nephilim — giant hybrid beings. Key leaders include Azazel (taught men warfare & women beautification), Araqiel, Kokabiel, Tamiel, and Baraqijal. Their corruption triggered the flood judgment described in Genesis 6. This event is the backstory Genesis leaves mysterious.
Book of Enoch
Azazel — The Scapegoat Connection
Azazel receives special punishment — bound hand and foot, cast into the desert of Dudael under sharp rocks. Enoch 10:8: "the whole earth has been corrupted through the works that were taught by Azazel: to him ascribe all sin." This directly parallels Leviticus 16 where the scapegoat is sent "to Azazel" into the wilderness — a connection that transforms our understanding of that ritual.
Book of Enoch
The Son of Man — Messianic Prophecy
Chapters 37–71 (The Book of Parables) contain remarkable pre-Christian descriptions of a "Son of Man" seated on God's throne, who will judge the world and vindicate the righteous. Jesus uses this exact title for himself throughout the Gospels. Multiple copies of Enoch were found among the Dead Sea Scrolls, confirming it was widely read in first century Judea — making the connection impossible to ignore.
Dead Sea Scrolls
Why Enoch Was Excluded from Canon
Enoch was widely accepted as scripture by early Jews and Christians — Jude 1:14-15 in the New Testament directly quotes it. Multiple copies were found at Qumran (c.150 BC – 70 AD). It was excluded at the Councils of Laodicea (363 AD) and Carthage (397 AD), likely because its detailed angelology complicated emerging doctrine. It remains canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Church today.
Scripture
Genesis 6 — The Nephilim
"There were giants in the earth in those days; and also after that, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men..." (Genesis 6:4 KJV). The Hebrew Nephilim (נְפִילִים) means "fallen ones." Enoch fills in exactly who the "sons of God" were and what happened — completing a narrative Genesis deliberately leaves incomplete.
Scripture
Jude's Direct Quote of Enoch
Jude 1:14-15 is a near-direct quote from 1 Enoch 1:9 — the only direct New Testament quotation from a non-canonical book. This confirms the early church viewed Enoch as authoritative prophetic scripture, raising serious questions about why it was later removed from the canon that Jude himself considered scripture.
Dead Sea Scrolls
The Discovery — Qumran 1947
Over 900 manuscripts discovered 1947–1956 in caves near the Dead Sea, dating from 3rd century BC to 1st century AD. Every Old Testament book except Esther was found. Critically, multiple copies of 1 Enoch and the Book of Giants were present — proving the Qumran community (likely Essenes) revered Enoch as sacred. The scrolls revolutionised our understanding of Second Temple Judaism.
